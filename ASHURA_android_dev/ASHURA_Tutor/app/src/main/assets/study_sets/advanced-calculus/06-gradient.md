# Gradient

In vector calculus, the gradient ∇f of a scalar-valued differentiable function f of several variables is the vector field (or vector-valued function) whose value at a point p gives the direction and the rate of fastest increase. The nabla symbol ∇, pronounced "del", denotes the vector differential operator.
If the gradient of a function is non-zero at a point p, the direction of the gradient is the direction in which the function increases most quickly from p, and the magnitude of the gradient is the rate of increase in that direction, the greatest absolute directional derivative.
This property of the gradient as the fastest increase leads to gradient descent, an optimization technique to minimize a function. This technique is widely used in machine learning.
In coordinate-free terms, the gradient ∇f may be defined by:
df=∇f⋅d𝐫
where df is the total infinitesimal change in f for an infinitesimal displacement d𝐫, and is seen to be maximal when d𝐫 is in the direction of the gradient ∇f.
In a Cartesian coordinate system, the gradient is a vector of partial derivatives. For f:ℝn→ℝ, its gradient ∇f:ℝn→ℝn is defined at the point p=(x1,…,xn) as the vector
∇f(p)=[∂f∂x1(p)⋮∂f∂xn(p)].
(See § Cartesian coordinates for the conditions under which the above formula applies).
The gradient is dual to the total derivative df; see § Relationship with total derivative. It also admits multiple generalizations to more general functions on manifolds; see § Generalizations.
