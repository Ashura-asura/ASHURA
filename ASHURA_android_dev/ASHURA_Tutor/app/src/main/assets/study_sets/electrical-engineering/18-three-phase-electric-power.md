# Three-phase electric power

Three-phase electric power (abbreviated 3ϕ) is the most widely used form of alternating current (AC) for electricity generation, transmission, and distribution. It is a type of polyphase system that uses three live wires, and no neutral wire in long transmission lines, thus being most economical and the standard method by which electrical grids deliver power around the world.
Due to the time shift between phases, each live wire current is always cancelled out to zero by the sum of the other two, thus a common neutral wire does not carry any current if the loads are properly balanced, e.g. when a generator powers a motor locally, or in case of large transmissions grids, in which loads can be balanced closely enough. In most 3-phase cables used eg. within buildings, a fourth wire is used as a neutral return for imbalanced loads, and a fifth as protective earth.
In a three-phase system, each of the three phases is offset by 120 degrees of phase shift relative to the others. This arrangement produces a more constant flow of power compared with single-phase systems, making it especially efficient for transmitting electricity over long distances and for powering heavy loads such as industrial machinery. Because it is an AC system, voltages can be easily increased or decreased with transformers, allowing high-voltage transmission and low-voltage distribution with minimal loss.
Three-phase circuits are also more economical: a three-wire system can transmit more power than a two-wire single-phase system of the same phase-to-phase voltage while using less conductor material. Beyond transmission, three-phase power is commonly used to run large induction motors, other electric motors, and heavy industrial loads, while smaller devices and household equipment often rely on single-phase circuits derived from the same network.
Three-phase electrical power was first developed in the 1880s by several inventors and has remained the backbone of modern electrical systems ever since, apart from an increasing amount of high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission systems being preferred for very long interconnectors recently.
